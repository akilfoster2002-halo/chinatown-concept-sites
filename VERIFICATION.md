# What is verified, and what is not

All seven sites were researched and built on **25 October 2026**. Every
business's Google Business listing was opened individually and read by hand.

> **These are concept sites.** None of these seven businesses has
> commissioned, seen or approved one. Every page says so, and every page is
> `noindex`.

## 2. No prices anywhere, on any site

This is the single biggest decision in the project, so it is worth stating
plainly: **not one of the seven publishes a price list.**

What exists instead, and why none of it was used:

- **Old blog posts.** Yue Lai's buns were $1.50 in a 2022 write-up. That is
  four years old.
- **Individual reviews.** A reviewer put Tao Hong's egg tart at $1.75 in
  May 2026. One person's receipt is not a price list.
- **A 2024 guide** quoted six buns for $4 at Fu Zhou Wei Zhong Wei.
- **Delivery apps.** Happy Star has an Uber Eats page with prices on it.
  Delivery prices are marked up and are not counter prices.

Every item on every site reads **"Ask at the counter"**.

## 3. Open questions, per business

These are printed on each site too, in a section called *What this site does
not claim to know*. They are the things to settle in a first phone call.

### Shui Mei Cafe — the name is genuinely unsettled
The most serious one. Google says **Shui Mei Cafe**. A 2026 reviewer writes
that the same business now trades as **QQ Cafe**. The awning in Google's own
photographs reads differently from both. Directories also list a second phone
number, **(212) 791-2819**, against the same address. *Ask what they call
themselves before anything else.*

### Yue Lai Bakery — Google has the wrong photo
At least one photograph filed under this business on Google shows a storefront
at **160 E Broadway**, which is Happy Star, a different shop. Nothing on the
Yue Lai site describes its appearance, because the photographic evidence is
not trustworthy. A second number, (212) 227-1617, is also listed elsewhere.

### Happy Star — it is not quite website-less
Google now shows an **"Order online"** button pointing at Uber Eats. It still
has no website, but it is one step less cold than the others. Its 3.7 from 114
reviews is also a genuinely divided verdict — read the one-star reviews before
approaching.

### Payment policy — unconfirmed for six of seven
Only **Fu Zhou Wei Zhong Wei** has cash-only stated clearly enough to publish
(18 reviews say so, independently). For the other six, third-party listings
and reviews contradict each other, so no payment line appears on those sites.

### Hours — one wide disagreement
**M&W Bakery:** Google says 7 am – 5:30 pm; Apple Maps says 8:30 am – 8 pm.
That is a two-and-a-half-hour gap at the end of the day. Google's is used.

### Tao Hong — seating
One review says four seats, another says none. Neither claim is made.

### Menus — all seven are incomplete
Not one of these is a full menu, and no site claims otherwise. Each list is
assembled from what reviewers name, which is always a fraction of what a
bakery case or a wall menu actually holds.

## 4. What is not on these sites, deliberately

- **No photographs.** Every usable image of these businesses belongs to a
  Google reviewer or a publication. They were used as reference for colour and
  art direction — which is what a photograph of a storefront is for — but none
  is reproduced. Stock food photography would imply it is their food, so there
  is none of that either. Each site carries a drawn SVG instead.
- **No schema.org structured data.** Publishing `LocalBusiness` markup for a
  business that has not asked would hand search engines a claim this site has
  no standing to make. Switch it on when there is a client.
- **No social links, no ordering links, no menus presented as official.**
